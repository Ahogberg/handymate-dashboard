# Handymate Pricing Strategy

**Status:** Strategic working document  
**Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Primary target segment:** Swedish trade/construction companies with approximately 5–20 employees

## 1. Strategic pricing thesis

Handymate should not be positioned or priced as a traditional CRM with AI features.

The long-term category is closer to:

> An operational intelligence layer that actively helps a trade company win more work, protect margin, invoice faster, reduce administration and surface risks before they become expensive.

The more Handymate can prove value in concrete SEK, saved time and reduced risk, the less relevant conventional per-seat SaaS pricing becomes.

The central commercial principle should be:

> Price Handymate against the economic value it creates and the operational work it removes — not against the cheapest CRM alternative.

## 2. Current launch pricing

Current preliminary launch pricing:

| Segment | Launch price |
|---|---:|
| Small company / up to roughly 5 employees | 5,990 SEK/month |
| Larger company / 5+ employees | 11,990 SEK/month |

These prices should be viewed primarily as **launch / market-entry pricing**, not necessarily as Handymate's permanent long-term ceiling.

Annualized:

- 5,990 SEK/month = 71,880 SEK/year
- 11,990 SEK/month = 143,880 SEK/year

For a generic software tool these may feel expensive.

For a system that can demonstrably recover missed revenue, protect project margin, shorten invoice cycles, improve quote follow-up and remove owner/admin work, they can become very inexpensive relative to value created.

## 3. Why the current launch price makes sense

The immediate objective at launch is not to maximize ARPA from the first cohort.

The priority is to acquire enough real companies to establish:

- measurable customer ROI
- case studies
- product usage patterns
- proof of Revenue Recovery
- proof of Offer-to-Reality value
- evidence of administrative time reduction
- retention data
- willingness to delegate work to agents

A strong launch price helps Handymate reach the first meaningful customer milestones faster while still positioning the product as serious business infrastructure rather than a cheap utility.

The strategic sequence should be:

```text
10 customers
→ 25
→ 50
→ 100
→ robust ROI evidence
→ higher pricing for new customers
```

## 4. Sweet spot: approximately 5–20 employees

Handymate's likely strongest initial customer profile is an owner-led trade/construction company with roughly 5–20 employees.

This segment is attractive because:

- the owner is still close to sales, projects and cashflow
- administrative pain is already substantial
- several jobs run in parallel
- missed follow-ups and missed invoicing become economically meaningful
- ÄTA, material, time and project-margin leakage matter
- the company may have an administrator or project manager, but still relies heavily on the owner
- buying decisions are comparatively fast
- ROI can become visible quickly

A 2–3 person company may love Handymate but has a lower absolute value ceiling.

A 10–20 person company can generate dramatically greater economic value because small percentage improvements apply to a much larger revenue base.

## 5. The key pricing reframing

Handymate should avoid being framed as:

> "A system costing 5,990–11,990 SEK per month."

Instead, the customer should increasingly experience:

> "A digital team that continuously finds money, prevents leakage and removes work from the owner."

Illustrative monthly value story:

- Lisa recovers a stalled quote: +64,000 SEK won work
- Project intelligence identifies unbilled ÄTA: +19,000 SEK
- Karin identifies 43,000 SEK that can be invoiced sooner
- Matte reduces 7 hours of administrative work

In that context, even one strong monthly intervention can finance a meaningful share of the annual subscription.

## 6. Value visualization is part of pricing strategy

Pricing power depends heavily on Handymate making value visible inside the product.

Handymate should distinguish between:

### Realized value

Value tied to an observable completed outcome.

Examples:

- recovered revenue actually invoiced
- invoice actually paid
- quote accepted after Handymate follow-up
- approved ÄTA converted to invoice

### Identified potential

Economic opportunity detected but not yet realized.

Examples:

- potentially unbilled work
- quote worth following up
- margin at risk
- overdue receivable

### Estimated time saved

Operational work Handymate plausibly removed or accelerated.

This must remain transparent and conservative.

Never mix these categories into one misleading ROI number.

## 8. Future pricing direction

Once real customer evidence proves the product consistently creates value, new-customer pricing can move materially upward.

Illustrative future structure — not a committed price list:

| Company size | Launch / early stage | Potential mature pricing |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1–5 employees | 5,990 | 7,990–9,990 SEK/month |
| 6–15 employees | 11,990 | 14,990–19,990 SEK/month |
| 16–30 employees | To define | 24,990–34,990 SEK/month |
| 30+ employees | Custom | 40,000+ SEK/month / custom |

These figures should not be adopted automatically.

They are a direction based on expected value creation and should only be activated when supported by:

- demonstrated ROI
- retention
- case studies
- product reliability
- clear willingness to pay
- segment-specific value

## 9. Why the current 5+ tier should eventually be split

A single `5+ employees` tier is likely too broad long-term.

A company with 6 employees and a company with 45 employees can receive radically different absolute value from Handymate.

A more mature segmentation could become:

### Handymate Core — 1–5 employees

Owner-led company where the owner still performs much of sales, project coordination and administration.

### Handymate Growth — 6–15 employees

Several parallel projects, more administration and higher value from margin, planning, follow-up and financial intelligence.

### Handymate Scale — 16–40 employees

Multiple project managers / supervisors, larger invoice flows, more complex operations and substantially higher value ceiling.

### Enterprise — 40+

Custom onboarding, integrations, governance, support, SLA and potentially more advanced autonomy controls.

The names are placeholders; the segmentation principle matters more than the labels.

## 10. Founding-customer pricing

A strong early commercial offer could be:

> Founding Customer Pricing — keep your launch price for as long as you remain a customer.

This gives the early buyer an immediate reason to move now while allowing Handymate to increase prices for later cohorts.

Example:

- early customer joins at 5,990 SEK
- mature new-customer price later becomes 8,990 SEK
- early customer retains their original price under defined terms

Benefits:

- stronger urgency
- easier early sales
- goodwill with first customers
- permission to raise future pricing without surprising the initial cohort

Avoid overly broad contractual promises that prevent future packaging changes; define exactly what is price-protected.

## 11. Do not price the roadmap

A critical principle:

> Customers pay for realized product value, not future roadmap ambition.

Handymate should not increase pricing aggressively merely because the architecture, agents or moat roadmap are impressive.

Price increases should follow actual customer evidence.

Required proof can include:

- Revenue Recovery precision
- SEK recovered
- faster invoice cycles
- administrative hours reduced
- margin leakage prevented
- quote win/follow-up improvements
- high retention
- agent actions customers repeatedly approve

## 12. Potential mature positioning

As Revenue Recovery, Offer-to-Reality, Karin, Project intelligence, dynamic agent actions and outcome tracking mature, the pricing conversation should shift away from competitor seat prices.

The commercial story becomes:

> Give Handymate a fraction of the economic value it creates, and it continuously works to protect and grow the business.

This is a much stronger category than:

> CRM for tradespeople.

## 13. Pricing moat through measurable outcomes

Handymate should capture the path:

```text
Detected opportunity
→ recommended action
→ user / agent action
→ business outcome
→ verified economic value
```

Examples:

```text
Unfollowed quote
→ follow-up sent
→ quote accepted
→ 68,400 SEK won work
```

```text
Potential unbilled ÄTA
→ owner reviews
→ ÄTA approved
→ invoice created
→ 8,900 SEK recovered
```

This outcome history supports:

1. product improvement
2. customer retention
3. stronger sales demos
4. case studies
5. pricing power
6. future outcome intelligence moat

## 14. Pricing guardrails

Handymate should follow these rules:

1. **Do not underprice large customers simply because the product is SaaS.**
2. **Do not increase prices before the value is reliable and visible.**
3. **Do not use misleading ROI calculations.**
4. **Keep realized and potential value separate.**
5. **Do not rely only on employee count forever.** Company complexity, project volume and economic value may later matter.
6. **Avoid pure per-seat pricing as the strategic center.** It conflicts with the value story and can discourage broad adoption.
7. **Do not charge a percentage of recovered revenue in V1 unless evidence later strongly supports that model.** A predictable subscription is easier for the initial market.
8. **Use customer evidence to determine the next price increase.**

## 15. When to raise pricing

Do not choose a date arbitrarily.

Raise pricing for new customers when several of the following become true:

- first strong case studies exist
- Revenue Recovery repeatedly identifies real value with acceptable false-positive rates
- customers can see realized Handymate value inside the product
- quote/project/invoice workflows are reliable
- agents generate actions customers trust
- churn signals are healthy
- multiple customers show ROI materially above subscription cost
- sales conversations increasingly focus on value rather than feature comparison

At that point, test increases incrementally rather than doubling pricing overnight.

Possible sequence:

```text
5,990 / 11,990
→ validate
→ 7,990 / 14,990 for new customers
→ validate
→ introduce Growth / Scale segmentation
→ continue value-based testing
```

## 16. Core commercial metric

A strategically important metric should become:

> Verified customer economic value / subscription cost

This can be complemented by:

- identified potential value
- time saved
- recovered revenue
- protected margin
- invoice-cycle improvement
- quote conversion influence

The purpose is not to create a vanity ROI number.

The purpose is to understand whether Handymate is priced as a rational share of the value customers actually receive.

## 17. Current recommendation

For launch:

- retain the preliminary 5,990 / 11,990 SEK pricing unless real sales evidence argues otherwise
- treat this explicitly as early-market pricing
- focus primarily on companies with approximately 5–20 employees
- consider a well-defined Founding Customer price-protection offer
- build value visualization into the product
- collect economic outcomes from the first cohort
- do not prematurely optimize the pricing model

After real customer evidence:

- split the broad `5+` segment
- raise new-customer pricing
- move toward value-based packaging
- ensure larger firms pay in proportion to the much greater value ceiling

## 18. Long-term principle

The long-term pricing question should not be:

> "How much can we charge for CRM software?"

It should become:

> "What is a fair and commercially optimal price for an operational intelligence system that can create hundreds of thousands of SEK in measurable annual value for this company?"

If Handymate succeeds in proving that value consistently, 5,990–11,990 SEK/month should be understood as an attractive early price — not the natural ceiling of the product.
